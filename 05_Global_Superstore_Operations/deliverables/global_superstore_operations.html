01 — Methodology

End-to-End ETL Pipeline

From a raw multi-sheet Excel file to a queryable SQLite database. Every transformation documented and reproducible.

STEP 01
📥

Extract

Ingested three sheets from a single .xls source file via pd.read_excel(): Orders (51,290 rows), Returns (1,173 rows), and People (14 rows).

STEP 02
🔧

Transform

Merged sheets in pandas, cleaned nulls and type inconsistencies, and engineered a ship_time column from Order Date and Ship Date for duration analysis.

STEP 03
🗄️

Load

Loaded into a SQLite database with three relational tables, establishing a clean analytical layer separable from raw source data.

STEP 04
🔍

Query

Four dedicated SQL scripts answered each business question. Python visualizations and a Power BI executive dashboard surfaced the findings.

03 — Key Findings

Four Questions.
Four Answers.

Each finding maps to a business question from the COO brief. Findings are observational, not proof of cause.

Q1 — Shipping Mode & Cost

Heavy Discounting, Not Shipping Cost, Separates Loss-Making Orders

24.5% of order lines lose money, and 13 lose more than $2,000 each. Loss-making lines carry an average discount of 45%, against 4% on profitable lines. Shipping cost as a share of sales is the same (10.9%) in both groups, and loss-making lines are spread evenly across segments (24% to 25% each). The ten largest losses all carried discounts of 50% to 80%.

45% vs 4% avg discount, loss-making vs profitable lines
Q2 — Revenue & Profit Leaders

Central Leads Profit by 60%, but on Volume, Not Margin

Central earned $311K in profit against $195K for second-place North, a 60% lead. But Central has 2.3x North's order lines and earns less on each: $28 per line vs $41, and an 11.0% margin vs 15.6%. North Asia has the highest margin (19.5%). "Central" is also three markets grouped together (EU, LATAM and US), so the lead reflects a large, mixed region rather than a single operating practice.

11.0% vs 15.6% profit margin, Central vs North
Q3 — Regional Return Rates

Two Regions Return at About Double the Rate, a Third at 1.7x

Return records exist for four of the seven markets (US, LATAM, APAC, EU). In those markets 5.8% of orders were returned. North Asia (13.8%) and West (11.7%) return at about twice that rate, and North (10.0%) at 1.7x. Every other region with return data sits between 2% and 5%. EMEA, Africa and Canada have no return records, so their return rates are unknown, not zero.

13.8% order return rate in North Asia (West 11.7%, North 10.0%)
Order-level benchmark: 1,172 returned of 20,140 orders (5.8%) in the four markets with return data.
Q4 — Correlational Analysis

High Shipping Cost Does Not Predict Returns

Of 12,078 order lines with above-average shipping cost ($26.38), 824 were on returned orders. Their profile matches all high-cost lines: 66% sold at $721 or less (66% for all high-cost lines) and 53% carried no discount (54% for all). High-cost lines also return at nearly the same rate as the rest (7.9% vs 7.2% in covered markets). Returned orders are not distinguishable by price, discount or shipping cost in this extract.

7.9% vs 7.2% return rate, high vs other shipping-cost lines
Analytical note: these cuts do not identify a return driver. Product, supplier and delivery-condition data would be needed.
04 — Recommendations

Four Questions.
Four Moves for the COO.

Each recommendation ties to a finding and should be piloted and measured before rollout.

1

Audit Discount Approval Rules

Loss-making lines average a 45% discount and the ten largest losses carried 50% to 80% discounts. Review who can approve deep discounts, starting with technology and office supplies orders, and set a margin floor. Measure the change in loss-making share (24.5% today).

Driven by Q1
2

Compare Regions on Margin, Not Total Profit

Central's lead comes from volume at a lower margin. Split the merged "Central" region by market, then compare regions on margin and profit per order. North Asia, with the highest margin (19.5%), is the better candidate to study for practices worth copying.

Driven by Q2
3

Extend Return Tracking, Then Survey High-Return Regions

Return records cover only four of seven markets. Extend tracking to EMEA, Africa and Canada, then run a structured return survey in North Asia and West, where order return rates are about double the rest. Transaction records cannot show why customers return.

Driven by Q3
4

Look Beyond Price and Shipping for Return Drivers

Price, discount and shipping cost do not separate returned orders from others. Bring in product category, supplier and delivery-condition data and test those before prescribing a return-reduction strategy.

Driven by Q4
