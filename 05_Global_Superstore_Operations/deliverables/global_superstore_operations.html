01 — Methodology

End-to-End ETL Pipeline

From a raw multi-sheet Excel file to a queryable SQLite database. Every transformation documented and reproducible.

STEP 01
📥

Extract

Ingested three sheets from a single .xls source file via pd.read_excel(): Orders (51,291 rows), Returns (1,174 rows), and People (14 rows).

STEP 02
🔧

Transform

Merged sheets in pandas, cleaned nulls and type inconsistencies, and engineered a ship_time column from Order Date and Ship Date for duration analysis.

STEP 03
🗄️

Load

Loaded into a SQLite database with three relational tables, establishing a clean analytical layer separable from raw source data.

STEP 04
🔍

Query

Four dedicated SQL scripts answered each business question. Python visualizations and a Power BI executive dashboard surfaced the findings.

03 — Key Findings

Four Questions.
Four Answers.

Each finding maps directly to a business question from the COO brief. No descriptive filler. Root cause only.

Q1 — Shipping Mode & Cost

Profit Losses Exceeding $2,000 Per Order Across All Categories

Shipping mode analysis reveals large profit losses found in every product category, some exceeding $2,000 per order. Shipping costs are not being recovered through pricing. This is a structural mismatch between ship mode selection and margin expectations, concentrated in the Corporate segment.

$2,000+ per-order losses found across categories
Q2 — Revenue & Profit Leaders

Central Region Leads Profit by 50% Over Second Place

Central is the outright profit leader, running 50% above the second-place region, North. That margin of outperformance is wide enough to be structural, not incidental. Central is doing something operationally that other regions are not, and leadership needs to know what it is before trying to replicate it.

+50% Central profit lead over second-place North
Q3 — Regional Return Rates

Three Regions Running at Double the Company Return Rate

The company-wide return rate is 5.96%. North Asia, North, and West all exceed 2× that average when analyzed by both product category and customer segment. The pattern holds across cuts. This is not a category anomaly or a segment quirk. It is regional and structural.

2× company avg: North Asia, North & West
Company return rate benchmark: 5.96% across 51,291 orders.
Q4 — Correlational Analysis

66% of High-Cost Returns Sold at or Below Average Price

Of 824 orders with both high shipping costs and returns, 542 (~66%) were sales made at or below the company average sale price of $721. Additionally, 436 of those 824 (~53%) were sold at full price with no discount applied, suggesting perceived value rather than price sensitivity may be driving return behavior.

824 high-cost returns studied
Analytical note: Q4 identifies co-occurring factors, not confirmed causes. These patterns warrant further investigation before prescribing operational changes.
04 — Recommendations

Four Questions.
Four Moves for the COO.

Every recommendation is traceable to a finding. No assumptions introduced at the recommendation stage.

1

Commission a Shipping Cost Pricing Study

Per-order losses exceeding $2,000 indicate that shipping costs are not being factored into pricing for corporate accounts. A formal pricing study across all ship modes and segments would establish a cost structure that closes the margin gap rather than absorbing it as an operating loss.

Driven by Q1
2

Conduct an Operational Review of the Central Region

Central's 50% profit lead over the next-best region is too large to be coincidental. An operational review would identify the specific practices driving that outperformance, whether in fulfillment, pricing, customer mix, or cost control,, and assess which of those practices can be replicated in underperforming regions.

Driven by Q2
3

Deploy a Return Survey in North Asia, North & West

Three regions are returning products at twice the company average. The data identifies where the problem is concentrated but cannot determine why from transaction records alone. A structured return survey in these three regions would surface root causes (product quality, delivery condition, mismatched expectations, or regional fulfillment issues) and inform a targeted reduction strategy.

Driven by Q3
4

Evaluate a Discount Strategy as a Perceived Value Intervention

53% of high-cost returns were sold at full price with no discount. If customers paying full price are still returning at elevated rates on high-shipping-cost items, perceived value at the point of purchase may be the driver. A targeted discount or value-add strategy on these items could reduce return rates by making the purchase feel more deliberate, without cutting into margin on every transaction.

Driven by Q4
