01 · The Problem

The business assumed views meant leads. The data disagreed.

The client — an online data analytics educator — was investing heavily in YouTube content and paid advertising to drive leads into her bootcamp program. But there was no clear picture of which content actually converted, which channels were truly pulling weight, and what the 32% of unattributed leads represented as a strategic blind spot.

The question wasn't just "what's working?" It was "what does working actually mean when views and lead volume tell completely different stories?"

Surface-level assumption

More views = more leads. High-performing content is identifiable by its view count. YouTube and paid ads serve separate purposes.

What the data revealed

Views and lead conversion were structurally decoupled. Two videos with near-identical view counts produced 422 vs. 66 leads respectively — a 6× gap demanding explanation.

02 · The Data

Four datasets. One cross-file bridge built on a naming convention.

The core analytical challenge was that no single dataset answered the business question. The value was in joining them — and the join key was hiding in plain sight: Hyros attribution tags embedded video publish dates in their source strings (e.g., @yt_10_2_2025), creating a bridge between lead data and YouTube performance.

03 · Key Findings

Six discoveries that changed the strategic picture.

1

Views do not predict leads

The Oct 2 video (422 leads) and an Oct 20 video (66 leads) had nearly identical view counts. Conversion wasn't about reach — it was about content intent and the audience state at the time of viewing.

2

YouTube drives ~66% of attributed leads

When stripping unattributed leads, YouTube's share of identifiable lead generation dominates every other channel combined — making it the engine of the business, not just a brand-building exercise.

3

Content is heavily "Both"-weighted — a structural vulnerability

Most top-performing videos function as both the First Touch and Last Touch simultaneously. Viewers arrive, decide, and book — all from one video. There is no Validator content reinforcing certainty between those steps, creating funnel fragility.

4

Top Closer content shares a replicable title pattern

High First-Source-converting videos disproportionately use FOMO framing and authority/trust signals in their titles. This is a concrete, low-cost content strategy lever.

5

32% of leads have no attribution — a data infrastructure gap

Nearly a third of all leads cannot be traced to any source. This isn't a small rounding error. It is a structural blind spot that limits the reliability of every attribution conclusion drawn from the remaining 68%.

6

Paid ads generated 113 high-intent leads at ~$140 cost-per-lead

The $15,884 spent on ads producing Hyros "Call" leads reveals a measurable — and improvable — paid acquisition cost. This benchmarks future ad efficiency conversations with the client.

"A finding with missing data infrastructure is a hypothesis, not a confirmed recommendation." The discipline of distinguishing what the data confirms versus what it suggests was the analytical backbone of this engagement.

04 · Methodology

How the analysis was structured and executed.

Data audit and join architecture

Identified the Hyros-to-YouTube bridge via source tag naming convention. Mapped join keys across all four files before writing a single query. Flagged the 32% attribution gap upfront as a data quality constraint on the entire analysis.

Outlier handling before analysis

The Oct 2 viral video (422 leads) was excluded from correlation analysis before seeing results — an analytically honest decision made in advance to prevent outlier-driven conclusions from contaminating the model.

Content role taxonomy

Categorized each YouTube video as Closer (First Source dominant), Validator (Last Source dominant), or Both — after First/Last Source definitions were clarified directly with the client stakeholder mid-project, which shifted the entire analytical frame.

Captivation Indicator development

Built a composite content scoring metric combining CTR and average view duration as a production guidance tool — helping the client understand what early content signals predict future engagement, even if they don't directly predict leads.

Tableau dashboard + executive presentation

Delivered a presentation-ready Tableau dashboard and a six-slide executive deck. The deck followed a Credibility → Vulnerability → Solution → Action narrative spine, framing the attribution gap not as a failure but as the strategic opportunity.

05 · Key Analytical Tool

The Captivation Indicator — a content production signal.

Rather than simply ranking videos by lead count, the analysis developed a forward-looking production guidance metric to help the client understand why certain content earns attention before conversion data is ever available.

Captivation Indicator = CTR × (Avg View Duration / Video Length)
CTRClick-through rate — signals title and thumbnail effectiveness at earning the click
View RatioAvg view duration ÷ video length — signals whether content holds the audience it earned
Score > 1.0Indicates above-average audience captivation across both dimensions

Important framing: the Captivation Indicator is a content creation compass, not a conversion predictor. Top lead-generating videos don't uniformly score above 1.0 — conversion depends on factors the metric doesn't capture. Its value is in guiding production decisions before outcomes are known.

06 · Recommendations

Two concrete, data-grounded actions for the client.

Highest impact

Mandatory enrollment survey

Implement a one-question "how did you hear about us?" survey at enrollment. Lowest barrier, highest information gain. Directly closes the 32% attribution gap over time without requiring new technology infrastructure.

Content strategy

Produce dedicated Validator content

The funnel currently has no middle. Create testimonial and authority-positioning videos that reinforce certainty for viewers who have already encountered the brand but haven't committed. Addresses the "Both"-content structural risk.

Content strategy

Replicate the Closer title formula

High First-Source-converting videos share FOMO and trust-signal framing in their titles. This pattern is replicable at zero additional cost — it only requires intentional title strategy, not new production resources.

Infrastructure

Evaluate SegMetrics or RB2B

Longer-term attribution infrastructure tools that could significantly reduce the unattributed lead volume. Prepared as a verbal recommendation for stakeholder discussion rather than a primary slide finding.

08 · What I Learned

The analytical instincts this project built.

Correlation ≠ causation is not just a rule — it's a discipline

Enforcing this in real data, under real time pressure, with a real stakeholder, is different from knowing it as a concept. Every finding was pressure-tested before it became a claim.

Stakeholder clarification changes everything

The First/Last Source redefinition mid-project invalidated the original analytical framework. Learning to rebuild cleanly — without ego — is a core consulting skill.

Data gaps are findings, not failures

The 32% attribution gap is the most actionable insight in the deck. Missing data infrastructure tells its own story — and it is often more valuable than the data that exists.

The narrative spine matters as much as the numbers

A Credibility → Vulnerability → Solution → Action structure isn't just presentation design. It's how analytical findings become strategic decisions that stakeholders actually act on.
