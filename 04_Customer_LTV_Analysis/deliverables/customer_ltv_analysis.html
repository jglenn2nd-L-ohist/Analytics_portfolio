02 — Dataset

One Year of Transactions.
Four Thousand Customers.

UCI Online Retail Dataset via Kaggle. December 2010 through December 2011. UK-based retailer with international reach.

Raw Entries
541,909

Entries across 8 columns before any cleaning. Included nulls, cancelled orders, non-customer records, and negative quantities requiring resolution before analysis.

Cleaned Records
392,692

Processable entries after six discrete cleaning operations. Cancelled orders, null Customer IDs, and non-product stock codes removed. Reduction rate: 27.6%.

Unique Customers
4,338

Distinct customers identified across 37 countries. UK is the dominant market. International customers tracked separately for geographic segmentation analysis.

Observation Period
13 mo.

December 2010 through December 2011. Later cohorts have fewer observation periods by design, which shapes how their retention and LTV patterns are interpreted.

03 — Key Findings

What the Cohorts Revealed.

Four data-backed findings across customer behavior, geography, retention, and cohort performance.

Finding 01 — Cohort Performance

Early Cohorts Outperform on Both Retention and Spend

The December 2010 cohort leads all others on retention rate and average spend per transaction at £27 per month. Average spend has declined consistently across cohorts through the observation period, suggesting either market maturation or a shift in customer acquisition quality over time.

£27/mo Dec 2010 cohort avg spend
Finding 02 — Geographic Risk

Geographic Concentration Creates Revenue Fragility

Netherlands and EIRE dominate international revenue. EIRE's figures are driven by just 3 wholesale accounts. Volume and customer quality tell different stories. A single account exit in EIRE would produce a significant revenue drop that would not appear in customer count metrics.

3 wholesale accounts driving EIRE revenue
Finding 03 — Retention Pattern

The Second Purchase Is the Critical Inflection Point

Across all cohorts, the largest retention decline occurs between period 0 and period 1. Customers who do not make a second purchase within the first month are significantly less likely to return at all. The window between first and second purchase is where retention strategy has the highest leverage.

Period 0-1 sharpest retention drop across all cohorts
Finding 04 — Later Cohorts

Later Cohort Patterns Are Indeterminate

Cohorts from July 2011 onward show compressed, similar behavior with limited observation periods. Whether this reflects market saturation, seasonality, or data truncation cannot be determined from this dataset alone. These cohorts require additional observation time before conclusions can be drawn.

July 2011+ cohorts with indeterminate patterns
Analytical note: Later cohorts have fewer periods by design. Treating their compressed behavior as a trend would be premature. Further observation required.
04 — Implications

Three Moves
for Leadership.

Each implication is grounded in a specific data pattern. No recommendations disconnected from the findings.

1

Invest in Australia

Australia ranks 4th or 5th across all three geographic metrics consistently: total revenue, average spend per customer, and purchase frequency. That consistency across three independent measures signals an engaged customer base with room to grow, not a one-dimensional revenue spike. A targeted investment in the Australian market has a more reliable foundation than doubling down on EIRE, where revenue is concentrated in three accounts.

Driven by Finding 02
2

Investigate the August 2011 Cohort

The 2011-08 cohort shows 59% and 58% retention at periods 2 and 3, well above surrounding cohorts. That outperformance does not appear in cohorts immediately before or after it. Something specific happened in August 2011 that drove higher retention. Identifying what that was, whether a campaign, a product launch, a channel shift, or a customer segment mix, could provide a repeatable acquisition playbook.

Driven by Finding 01
3

Replace Email with Direct Outreach After First Purchase

The data shows period 0 to period 1 is where retention collapses. A phone-based contact campaign after first purchase addresses that window directly. The goal is to build a personal relationship before the customer goes dormant, making the second purchase a natural next step rather than a response to a generic marketing event. Email sequences have already proven insufficient at this inflection point.

Driven by Finding 03
