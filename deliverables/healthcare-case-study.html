03 — Key Findings

What the Data Found

Four distinct attrition drivers — each one a data-backed signal, not an assumption.

Finding 01 — Satisfaction

Unhappy Entry-Level Employees Leave at the Highest Rate

47% of unhappy entry-level employees left the company. Low satisfaction is the single strongest predictor of departure — especially among staff with the least tenure. These are employees in their most formative years with the organization.

47% of unhappy entry-level staff departed
Finding 02 — Involvement

Disconnected Employees Are the Highest Flight Risk

50% of those who left rated job involvement as "very little." Employees who feel disconnected from their work leave regardless of compensation — pointing to engagement as a structural problem, not an individual one.

50% rated job involvement as "very little"
Finding 03 — Income

A 33% Pay Gap Is Driving 77% of Departures

77% of departing employees earned less than $5,000/month. Regional nurses average $6,100/month — Watson Healthcare is offering $4,024. That 33% gap is the most concrete, actionable finding in the dataset.

$6,100 regional average vs. $4,024 offered
Finding 04 — Work-Life Balance

40% Cited Poor Work-Life Balance as a Factor

Those who were "very dissatisfied" with work-life balance left at the highest rate. Long hours and overtime without scheduling flexibility are pushing employees out — particularly among nurses who already carry heavy workloads.

40% cited unfavorable work-life balance
04 — Recommendations

Three Moves
for Leadership

Every recommendation ties directly to a finding. No guesswork. No filler.

1

Close the Pay Gap

Regional nurses earn 33% more than Watson Healthcare is offering. Bringing compensation in line with the $6,100/month regional average addresses the single largest measurable driver of attrition. Even partial movement toward market rate reduces the financial incentive to leave.

Driven by finding 03
2

Build a Mentor-Mentee Program

50% of attrition gave job involvement low marks. A structured mentor-mentee program where mentees receive consistent acknowledgment and feedback creates a clear signal that their contributions matter — directly improving job involvement scores and reducing the disengagement that precedes departure.

Driven by finding 02
3

Create Flexible Scheduling Options

40% of departing employees cited unfavorable work-life balance. Employees working long hours would benefit significantly from flexible scheduling — reducing burnout and directly addressing the overtime dissatisfaction that shows up consistently in the departure data.

Driven by finding 04
